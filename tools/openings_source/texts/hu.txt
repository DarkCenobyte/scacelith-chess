# Coach openings: Hungarian texts (keys of en.txt). tools/gen_opening_lang.py merges them with the names of
# families.json into assets/coach/openings/hu.lang.
# Register: the coach says "te" to the player (tegezés: "számíts", "vigyázz") and "én" for himself; Hungarian has no
# grammatical gender, so nothing agrees with the sex of the coach or of the player.
# Opening names: "def" is the name with its article, lower case except proper names ("a szicíliai védelem",
# "az angol megnyitás", "a Najdorf-változat"); the names fragment also gives every family and variation the case
# forms acc (-t), dat (-nak/-nek), ins (-val/-vel), ine (-ban/-ben), ela (-ból/-ből) and sub (-ra/-re), with the
# article ("az olasz játszmával"), so a template never attaches a suffix to a name. {line} stands alone after a
# colon. {n} is a move number said as an ordinal through the coach's count.ord ("a játszma 12. lépésénél",
# spoken "tizenkettedik").
# Squares never take a suffix or a hyphenated ending (the voice spells them, and "c3-at", "f7-et" would come out
# wrong): the case goes on a noun next to them ("az f3 mezőn", "a c4 mezőre", "az e6 lépéssel", "a d4
# előretolás", "a c5 áttörés", "e5 után"); a pawn on a square is written "az e4-gyalog". The article before a
# square follows the letter as said: "az" before a, e, f ("az e4", "az f3", "az a6"), "a" before the others.
# Files as "c-vonal", pawns as "d-gyalog" (file_pattern spells the letter); pieces in words, never piece
# letters. White and Black are "világos" and "sötét" (lower case inside a sentence); a light-squared bishop is
# "világosmezős futó", never "világos futó" (White's bishop).

# ---- Sentences. "pw" = the player (the listener) has White, "pb" = the player has Black; the coach is the
# other side. Gambits are "elfogadott" (accepted) or "elhárított" (declined), as in their Hungarian names.
