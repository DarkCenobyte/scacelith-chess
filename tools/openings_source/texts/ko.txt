# Coach openings: Korean texts (keys of en.txt). tools/gen_opening_lang.py merges them with the names of
# families.json into assets/coach/openings/ko.lang.
# The coach's warm polite haeyo-che (-요 endings, glossary/ko.md), with the honorific -시- on the player's own
# moves (시작하셨고, 응수하셨어요), never on the coach's (시작했고, 응수했어요). No pronoun for the player.
# Opening names end in varying sounds, so no particle is ever written after a name placeholder: the names
# fragment gives every family and variation the forms obj (을/를), ins (으로/로), cop (이에요/예요) and was
# (이었/였 + an ending, for other coach files); "def" is the bare name, used before the invariant particle 에 or
# in a list. {line} stands alone after a colon; {n} is followed by the counter 수째 (a move number).
# Squares as "e4", files as "c 파일", pawns as "d 폰" (the file letter is spelled through file_pattern); pieces
# in words. A particle after a written square follows its Sino-Korean reading, as the voice says it: rank 2, 4,
# 5 end in a vowel (d4로, e5를, c5와), 3 and 6 in a consonant (c3으로, e6을, a6과), 1, 7 and 8 in ㄹ (f7로,
# f7을). "the e-pawn" is said as the pawn's square (e5 폰): "이 폰" would sound like "this pawn".
# Short names in comments, as Korean players say them: 시실리안, 프렌치, 카로칸, 이탈리안, 잉글리시...
