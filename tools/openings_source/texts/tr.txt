# Coach openings: Turkish texts (keys of en.txt). tools/gen_opening_lang.py merges them with the names of
# families.json into assets/coach/openings/tr.lang.
# Register: the coach says "sen" to the player ("bekle", "dikkat et") and "ben" for himself; Turkish has no
# grammatical gender. White and Black as players are "Beyaz" and "Siyah", capitalised, suffixes without an
# apostrophe ("Siyahın", "Beyaza"); a light-squared bishop is "beyaz kareli fil" (lower case).
# Opening names: "def" is the citation name in title case ("Sicilya Savunması", "Najdorf Varyantı"). A name
# ends in varying sounds, so no suffix is ever written after a name placeholder: names stay in the nominative,
# as a subject or predicate, as a bare object of "oynamak" ("{both:def} oynuyoruz"), before the separate word
# "ile" ("{white:def} ile açtın"), or before a noun ("{opening:def} pozisyonlarına"). {line} stands alone after
# a colon. {n} is a move number said as an ordinal through the coach's count.ord ("{n:ord} hamlede": written
# "12. hamlede", spoken "on ikinci hamlede").
# Squares are written "e4"; a suffix after a written square follows the rank as said (dört, beş, altı...):
# "e4'e", "d5'te", "f7'yi", "c6'daki". Files as "c dikeyi", pawns as "d piyonu" (the file_pattern spells the
# letter); pieces in words, never piece letters. Opening names in the comments are the names of the fragment,
# inflected as Turkish writes them ("Sicilya'ya", "Vezir Gambitine", "Kapalı İspanyol'a").

# ---- Sentences. "pw" = the player (the listener) has White, "pb" = the player has Black; the coach is the
# other side. Declined and accepted always answer a gambit (Queen's or King's Gambit): "gambiti kabul etmek".
