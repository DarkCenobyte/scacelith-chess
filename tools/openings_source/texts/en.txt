# Coach openings: hand-written English texts (the reference language).
# tools/gen_opening_lang.py merges them with the names of families.json into assets/coach/openings/en.lang.
#
# Comments say what an opening typically leads to, short enough to be spoken in a few seconds. They name
# squares ("e4") and pawn moves by their square, and pieces in words ("the knight on f3"), never piece-letter
# notation: the generator spells squares and files out for speech ("ee four", "the see file"), which it can
# only do for these forms. English files: "the c-file", "the d-pawn".

# ---- Sentences. "pw" = the player (the listener) has White, "pb" = the player has Black; the coach is the
# other side. {white}, {black}, {both}, {variation}, {opening}: opening names ("def" = the form used inside a
# sentence, with its article); {line}: a composed lichess variation name (citation form); {n}: a move number.
opening.say.pw.both = You opened with {white:def}, and I answered with {black:def}.
opening.say.pw.both.2 = Your opening is {white:def}, and my answer is {black:def}.
opening.say.pb.both = I opened with {white:def}, and you answered with {black:def}.
opening.say.pb.both.2 = My opening is {white:def}, and your answer is {black:def}.
opening.say.pw.declined = You played {white:def}, and I declined it.
opening.say.pb.declined = I played {white:def}, and you declined it.
opening.say.pw.accepted = You played {white:def}, and I accepted it.
opening.say.pb.accepted = I played {white:def}, and you accepted it.
opening.say.pw.white = You opened with {white:def}.
opening.say.pw.white.2 = Your opening is {white:def}.
opening.say.pb.white = I opened with {white:def}.
opening.say.pb.white.2 = My opening is {white:def}.
opening.say.pw.black = I answered with {black:def}.
opening.say.pb.black = You answered with {black:def}.
opening.say.both = We are playing {both:def}.
opening.say.both.2 = This is {both:def}.
opening.say.variation = More precisely, this is {variation:def}.
opening.say.variations = More precisely: {white:def} for White, {black:def} for Black.
opening.say.now_variation = Now we are in {variation:def}.
opening.say.transposed = By transposition, we are now in {opening:def}.
opening.say.deep = The exact line: {line}.

opening.say.rare = This is a rare opening.
opening.say.rare_named = This is a rare opening: {line}.

# ---- Families: beginner (levels 1-3) and advanced (levels 4-6) comments
opening.family.kings_pawn.beginner = Pushing the king's pawn two squares grabs the centre and frees the queen and a bishop at once: the most popular first move.
opening.family.kings_pawn.advanced = e4 leads to open, tactical games after e5, and to sharp, unbalanced fights against the Sicilian, the French or the Caro-Kann.
opening.family.queens_pawn.beginner = The queen's pawn takes the centre on d4, where the queen already protects it: the games are usually slower and more strategic than after e4.
opening.family.queens_pawn.advanced = Queen's pawn openings lead to closed positions where piece placement, pawn structure and the c4 and e4 breaks matter more than early tactics.
opening.family.kings_knight.beginner = The knight comes out and attacks the e5 pawn: the most natural developing move, usually answered by defending with the other knight.
opening.family.kings_knight.advanced = The knight on f3 keeps every classical option open, the Ruy Lopez, the Italian, the Scotch or the Four Knights, and makes Black commit to a defence of e5.
opening.family.sicilian.beginner = Black answers with the c-pawn instead of the e-pawn and unbalances the game: expect a sharp fight, each side attacking on its own wing.
opening.family.sicilian.advanced = After d4 and the pawn trade, Black gets the half-open c-file and more centre pawns, while White gets faster development and kingside attacking chances.
opening.family.french.beginner = Black prepares d5 with e6: a solid defence, but the light-squared bishop is often shut in behind its own pawns.
opening.family.french.advanced = When White pushes on to e5, a locked pawn chain appears: White attacks on the kingside, Black strikes at the chain with c5 and f6.
opening.family.caro_kann.beginner = Black prepares d5 with c6 and keeps the light-squared bishop free: a very solid defence with few weaknesses.
opening.family.caro_kann.advanced = Black gets a healthy pawn structure and an active light-squared bishop; White takes space or a small lead in development, Black aims for c5 or a good endgame.
opening.family.scandinavian.beginner = Black attacks the e4 pawn at once with d5; when the queen recaptures, White gains time by chasing her, so she needs a safe square.
opening.family.scandinavian.advanced = A Caro-Kann-like structure with the light-squared bishop outside the pawn chain: White keeps a little more space, Black gets a solid, easy setup.
opening.family.italian.beginner = White puts the bishop on c4, aiming at the weak f7 square: a classical opening where both sides develop quickly and castle.
opening.family.italian.advanced = Modern Italian games are slow manoeuvring battles with c3 and d3 that prepare d4 later; the Evans Gambit and the knight jump to g5 are the sharp tries.
opening.family.ruy_lopez.beginner = White's bishop attacks the knight that defends e5: one of the oldest and deepest openings, rich in slow strategic play.
opening.family.ruy_lopez.advanced = White builds lasting pressure on e5 and the centre with c3 and d4, while Black gains queenside space with a6 and b5; the Marshall Attack is the sharp exception.
opening.family.scotch.beginner = White opens the centre at once with d4: the pieces come out fast and the position opens early.
opening.family.scotch.advanced = The central tension disappears at once; the fight is about Black's active development and the pawn structures after an exchange on c6.
opening.family.philidor.beginner = Black defends e5 with the d-pawn: solid but a little passive, because the dark-squared bishop is shut in.
opening.family.philidor.advanced = Black gives up space for a firm hold on e5; White enjoys comfortable central pressure, and tricks against f7 are common at club level.
opening.family.petrov.beginner = Black does not defend e5 but counterattacks e4 with the knight: a solid, symmetrical defence; just do not copy White's moves for too long.
opening.family.petrov.advanced = The Petrov leads to symmetrical, often drawish structures: White keeps a small initiative, Black develops quickly and aims for equalising exchanges.
opening.family.four_knights.beginner = Both sides bring out their knights first: a sound, quiet opening that follows the rules of development.
opening.family.four_knights.advanced = A symmetrical, balanced opening: White chooses a Spanish, Scotch or Italian treatment, and Black should know the Rubinstein countergambit.
opening.family.english.beginner = White starts with the c-pawn and controls d5 from the side: a flexible opening where the centre is fought for with pieces.
opening.family.english.advanced = The English often transposes to queen's pawn openings; its own lines bring fianchetto manoeuvres, pressure on d5 and queenside play.
opening.family.zukertort.beginner = Developing the king's knight first watches the centre without committing a pawn yet.
opening.family.zukertort.advanced = A move-order tool: it can become a Réti, an English, a Catalan or a Queen's Gambit while sidestepping some of Black's defences.
opening.family.queens_gambit.beginner = White offers the c-pawn to pull Black's pawn away from the centre; it is not a real sacrifice, because White can usually win it back.
opening.family.queens_gambit.advanced = c4 hits the d5 strongpoint: Black either holds it, as in the Declined or the Slav, or gives it up for free development, as in the Accepted.
opening.family.qgd.beginner = Black keeps the pawn on d5 and supports it with e6: very solid, but the light-squared bishop needs care.
opening.family.qgd.advanced = Classical structures: the Carlsbad with White's minority attack, Black freeing the game with c5 or e5, and the eternal problem of the light-squared bishop.
opening.family.qga.beginner = Black takes the pawn but does not try to keep it: the point is to develop freely and hit White's centre with c5.
opening.family.qga.advanced = White regains the pawn and often ends up with an isolated d-pawn: piece activity against a long-term structural weakness.
opening.family.slav.beginner = Black supports d5 with c6 and keeps the light-squared bishop free to come out: a very solid defence.
opening.family.slav.advanced = The Slav holds d5 without locking in the light-squared bishop; the main lines turn on Black taking on c4 and developing that bishop to f5 or g4.
opening.family.semi_slav.beginner = Black combines the Slav pawn on c6 with the pawn on e6: it looks quiet, but it leads to very sharp lines.
opening.family.semi_slav.advanced = The Semi-Slav branches into the Meran, with queenside expansion by b5, and the ultra-sharp Botvinnik and Moscow gambits.
opening.family.indian.beginner = Black answers with the knight instead of a pawn and keeps the options open: the gateway to all the Indian defences.
opening.family.indian.advanced = The knight on f6 stops e4 for now; Black's next moves choose between the King's Indian, the Nimzo-Indian, the Queen's Indian, the Grünfeld or a Benoni.
opening.family.kings_indian.beginner = Black lets White build a big centre, puts the bishop on g7 and strikes back later: a dynamic fighting defence.
opening.family.kings_indian.advanced = Black hits the centre with e5 or c5; once it closes, White attacks on the queenside while Black storms the kingside with f5, f4 and g5.
opening.family.london.beginner = White sets up d4, the bishop on f4, e3, the knight on f3 and c3 against almost anything: easy to learn, solid, with a clear plan.
opening.family.london.advanced = A stable pawn pyramid with the bishop outside the chain: White plays for a kingside initiative or a slow squeeze, and an early c5 is Black's most testing reply.
opening.family.modern.beginner = Black puts the king's bishop on g7 with g6 and lets White take the centre, planning to attack it later.
opening.family.modern.advanced = Hypermodern and flexible: Black delays the knight to f6 and keeps c6, a6 and b5, or e5, in reserve while White builds a big centre.
opening.family.pirc.beginner = Black plays d6, the knight to f6 and g6, lets White build a centre, then counters it: a flexible fighting defence.
opening.family.pirc.advanced = White gets a broad centre; the main battles are the Austrian Attack with f4 and the attacks with queen and bishop, while Black strikes with c5 or e5.
opening.family.vienna.beginner = White develops the queen's knight first and often follows with f4: a lively opening with quick attacking chances.
opening.family.vienna.advanced = The Vienna prepares f4, the Vienna Gambit, or a quiet setup; Black's main answer is the knight to f6 with a central counter-strike by d5.
opening.family.bishops_opening.beginner = White develops the bishop to c4 before the knight, aiming at f7 and keeping the f-pawn free.
opening.family.bishops_opening.advanced = A flexible move order that often transposes to the Italian or the Vienna, with quiet d3 setups as its main independent idea.
opening.family.alekhine.beginner = Black attacks e4 with the knight to lure White's pawns forward: they gain space, but they can become targets.
opening.family.alekhine.advanced = Black invites the pawns forward, then undermines the overextended centre with d6 and c5; the calm Modern Variation is White's main line.
opening.family.kings_gambit.beginner = White sacrifices the f-pawn to open the f-file and take the centre: a romantic attacking opening from the nineteenth century.
opening.family.kings_gambit.advanced = White trades a pawn for the centre and the f-file; Black can hold the pawn with g5, give it back with d5, or decline the gambit.
opening.family.kga.beginner = Black takes the f-pawn: the game becomes very sharp, and both kings are a little exposed.
opening.family.kga.advanced = Black either tries to keep the pawn with g5 or gives it back for quick development; White relies on the f-file and a lead in development.
opening.family.kgd.beginner = Black refuses the pawn and develops instead, often with the bishop to c5, which keeps White from castling for a while.
opening.family.kgd.advanced = The bishop on c5 pins down White's kingside and the Falkbeer strikes back in the centre; both avoid the long forcing lines of the accepted gambit.
opening.family.nimzowitsch_def.beginner = Black answers with the queen's knight: an unusual but playable defence that often transposes to other openings.
opening.family.nimzowitsch_def.advanced = It invites structures with d4 and d5 or e5, or transposes to the open games; its value lies in surprise and flexible piece play.
opening.family.owen.beginner = Black puts the queen's bishop on b7 to hit e4 from afar; White should simply take the centre.
opening.family.owen.advanced = A hypermodern setup with b6, the bishop on b7 and e6 that gives up the centre early; White gains space and must watch the long diagonal.
opening.family.horwitz.beginner = Black plays e6 and keeps the options open: it usually turns into a French, a Queen's Gambit Declined or a Dutch.
opening.family.horwitz.advanced = A transpositional move order; its own lines are the Kangaroo, with a bishop check on b4, and English Defence setups with b6.
opening.family.vant_kruijs.beginner = Pushing the king's pawn one square opens the bishop but takes little of the centre: a quiet start.
opening.family.vant_kruijs.advanced = It usually transposes into reversed French, Colle or Stonewall setups and hands Black the fight for the centre at no real risk.
opening.family.center_game.beginner = White opens the centre with d4 on move two, but after the exchange the queen recaptures early and can be chased.
opening.family.center_game.advanced = Black develops with tempo against the queen; White usually brings her to e3, develops the queenside and castles long for an attack.
opening.family.englund.beginner = Black offers the e-pawn and hopes for traps: objectively dubious, but dangerous if White does not know it.
opening.family.englund.advanced = The gambit relies on queen tricks on e7 and b4; White keeps a sound extra pawn with calm, careful development.
opening.family.dutch.beginner = Black plays f5 to control e4 and aim at the kingside: ambitious, but it loosens Black's king a little.
opening.family.dutch.advanced = Leningrad, Stonewall or Classical setups: Black fights for e4 and a kingside attack, White aims at e4 and the weakened e6 square.
opening.family.nimzo_indian.beginner = Black pins the knight on c3 with the bishop to stop e4: a highly respected defence that often leaves White with doubled pawns.
opening.family.nimzo_indian.advanced = Black gives bishop for knight to control e4 and damage White's structure; White gets the bishop pair and central potential.
opening.family.queens_indian.beginner = Black puts the queen's bishop on b7 to control the central light squares: solid and flexible.
opening.family.queens_indian.advanced = Black fights for e4 and d5 with pieces; typical ideas are the bishop to a6 against c4, Hedgehog setups, and the c5 or d5 breaks.
opening.family.grunfeld.beginner = Black lets White build a big pawn centre, then attacks it with pieces and the bishop on g7: a dynamic, modern defence.
opening.family.grunfeld.advanced = In the Exchange lines White gets a big centre and Black hits d4 with the bishop, c5 and the knights: very concrete theory.
opening.family.catalan.beginner = White combines the Queen's Gambit with a bishop on g2 that presses on Black's queenside along the long diagonal.
opening.family.catalan.advanced = White gives up or delays the c4 pawn for pressure on the long diagonal, and the endgames often favour White's bishop.
opening.family.benoni.beginner = Black plays c5 and accepts a blocked centre in exchange for a queenside pawn majority: an ambitious, unbalanced defence.
opening.family.benoni.advanced = An asymmetrical structure: Black has the queenside majority and the bishop on g7, White the central majority and the e5 break.
opening.family.benko.beginner = Black gives a queenside pawn to open the a-file and the b-file for the rooks: long-lasting pressure rather than quick tactics.
opening.family.benko.advanced = A pawn for permanent queenside pressure from the rooks and the bishop on g7; Black is often happy in an endgame a pawn down.
opening.family.reti.beginner = White develops the knight, then attacks Black's centre from the side with c4: a flexible, hypermodern opening.
opening.family.reti.advanced = The Réti presses on d5 with c4 and a fianchetto on g2, often transposing to Catalan, English or Queen's Gambit structures.
opening.family.kings_indian_attack.beginner = White sets up the knight on f3, a fianchetto on g2, castling, d3 and e4 against almost anything: a system with a clear kingside plan.
opening.family.kings_indian_attack.advanced = A King's Indian with the colours reversed and an extra tempo; White often pushes to e5 and attacks the king while Black expands on the queenside.
opening.family.bird.beginner = White starts with the f-pawn to control e5: an unusual opening that leads to Dutch positions with the colours reversed.
opening.family.bird.advanced = White aims for a kingside setup with the bishop on b2 or a fianchetto on g2; Black's sharpest answer is From's Gambit with e5.
opening.family.ponziani.beginner = White prepares d4 with c3 to build a pawn centre: an old and fairly rare opening.
opening.family.ponziani.advanced = c3 prepares d4 but takes the knight's best square; Black equalises with a counterattack by the knight to f6 or by d5.
opening.family.three_knights.beginner = White brings out a third knight early; if Black adds the fourth knight, it becomes the Four Knights Game.
opening.family.three_knights.advanced = A transpositional move order; Black's independent tries are the bishop to b4 and a fianchetto with g6.
opening.family.danish.beginner = White gives one or even two pawns for a big lead in development and two bishops aiming at Black's king.
opening.family.danish.advanced = Pawns are traded for open lines and bishops on c4 and b2; giving material back with d5 is Black's safest answer.
opening.family.blackmar_diemer.beginner = White sacrifices a pawn with e4 and f3 to open the f-file and attack quickly.
opening.family.blackmar_diemer.advanced = A pawn for development and the half-open f-file; theory favours Black if Black develops soundly.
opening.family.trompowsky.beginner = White attacks the knight on f6 at once with the bishop: an early surprise that takes Black out of the usual paths.
opening.family.trompowsky.advanced = The bishop threatens to double Black's pawns; Black answers with the knight to e4, with e6 or with c5, and the play becomes unbalanced.
opening.family.torre.beginner = White develops the bishop to g5 to pin the knight and builds a solid centre with e3 and c3.
opening.family.torre.advanced = A system aiming for e4 with the bishop on g5; Black usually replies with c5 or an early h6.
opening.family.colle.beginner = White builds the pawn triangle d4, e3 and c3 with the bishop on d3 and prepares e4: a simple, solid attacking system.
opening.family.colle.advanced = White prepares e4 for a kingside attack, or develops the other bishop to b2; the drawback is the passive bishop on c1.
opening.family.tarrasch_def.beginner = Black plays c5 against the Queen's Gambit and accepts an isolated pawn in exchange for free piece play.
opening.family.tarrasch_def.advanced = Black deliberately takes an isolated queen's pawn for activity; White blockades d5 and fianchettoes, Black looks for piece play and the d4 push.
opening.family.budapest.beginner = Black gives the e-pawn to develop quickly; the pawn is usually won back.
opening.family.budapest.advanced = Black regains the pawn in most lines with the knight on g4; White keeps a small edge, and the Fajarowicz is a trickier sideline.
opening.family.bogo_indian.beginner = Black checks with the bishop on b4 to disturb White's development: a solid, simple defence.
opening.family.bogo_indian.advanced = Usually a bishop trade on d2 or a Nimzo-like structure without the pin; Black aims for a solid setup with d5, or with d6 and e5.
opening.family.old_indian.beginner = Black plays d6 and e5 without a fianchetto: a solid but slightly passive defence.
opening.family.old_indian.advanced = A King's Indian with the bishop on e7: solid but cramped, with central play based on e5.
opening.family.chigorin.beginner = Black develops the queen's knight to c6 against the Queen's Gambit and plays for active pieces.
opening.family.chigorin.advanced = Black gives up central solidity for piece activity, often trading the light-squared bishop for the knight on f3 to press on d4.
opening.family.albin.beginner = Black counter-sacrifices the e-pawn to push on to d4 and cramp White: sharp and full of traps.
opening.family.albin.advanced = Black relies on the pawn wedge on d4 and fast development; White's best is quiet development with a fianchetto, returning material when needed.
opening.family.latvian.beginner = Black plays f5 on move two: a very risky gambit that weakens Black's own king.
opening.family.latvian.advanced = Objectively dubious: White gets a strong initiative by taking on e5 or with the bishop on c4.
opening.family.elephant.beginner = Black strikes back with d5 at once and offers a pawn to open lines: risky but tricky.
opening.family.elephant.advanced = Objectively unsound: after the exchange on d5, White keeps a good game with careful development.
opening.family.nimzo_larsen.beginner = White puts the queen's bishop on the long diagonal after b3 to control e5 and the centre from afar.
opening.family.nimzo_larsen.advanced = A hypermodern flank setup: Black occupies the centre with e5 or d5, and White presses on it with the bishop, e3, and f4 or c4.
opening.family.polish.beginner = White starts with b4, an unusual wing move that gains space on the queenside.
opening.family.polish.advanced = The bishop goes to b2 behind the pawn; the b4 pawn can become a target, but the opening is playable and surprising.
opening.family.grob.beginner = White pushes the g-pawn two squares on move one: a provocative opening that weakens White's own king.
opening.family.grob.advanced = White aims at the long diagonal and b7; Black answers with d5 and e5 and should punish the loosened kingside.
opening.family.hungarian.beginner = White prepares a fianchetto with g3: a flexible start that often becomes a King's Indian Attack or an English.
opening.family.hungarian.advanced = Highly transpositional: Réti, English and King's Indian Attack setups, or a reversed Modern Defence.
opening.family.mieses.beginner = White starts very modestly with d3, opening the dark-squared bishop without taking the centre.
opening.family.mieses.advanced = It often leads to reversed Pirc or King's Indian Attack setups; Black may take the centre freely.
opening.family.saragossa.beginner = White starts with c3, a very rare and quiet first move.
opening.family.saragossa.advanced = c3 prepares a supported d4 and can transpose to reversed Caro-Kann or Slav structures.
opening.family.van_geet.beginner = White develops the queen's knight first, an unusual move that keeps e4 or d4 in reserve.
opening.family.van_geet.advanced = It often transposes to Vienna, reversed Sicilian or Pirc-type positions.
opening.family.jobava_london.beginner = White combines the knight on c3 with the bishop on f4: an aggressive system that aims at c7 with a knight jump to b5.
opening.family.jobava_london.advanced = White often castles long and attacks with h4 or g4; Black must watch c7 and the e4 advance.
opening.family.stonewall_attack.beginner = White puts pawns on d4, e3 and f4 and the bishop on d3, aiming for a kingside attack with a knight on e5.
opening.family.stonewall_attack.advanced = e5 is firmly controlled, but e4 and c4 become weak; Black's best is a quick c5 or trading off White's light-squared bishop.
opening.family.richter_veresov.beginner = White develops the queen's knight to c3 and the bishop to g5 early, aiming for e4.
opening.family.richter_veresov.advanced = The knight blocks the c-pawn but supports f3 and e4; Black does well with c5, or with the bishop to f5.
opening.family.st_george.beginner = Black starts with a6 and b5, a rare wing setup made famous by Miles's win over Karpov.
opening.family.st_george.advanced = Black aims for the bishop on b7 and queenside expansion and gives up the centre; White keeps an edge with simple central development.
opening.family.borg.beginner = Black pushes the g-pawn two squares: a provocative opening that weakens Black's own king.
opening.family.borg.advanced = The Grob with the colours reversed; White gains space and time with d4 and pressure on g5.
opening.family.hippopotamus.beginner = Black puts the pawns on the sixth rank and fianchettoes both bishops, waiting for White to overextend.
opening.family.hippopotamus.advanced = A flexible waiting setup: sound against slow play, cramped against a broad centre.
opening.family.english_defense.beginner = Black answers with b6 and the bishop on b7 to fight for e4 with pieces.
opening.family.english_defense.advanced = Black invites a big centre, then attacks it with a bishop check on b4, with f5 and with pressure on e4.
opening.family.pterodactyl.beginner = Black fianchettoes the king's bishop, then plays c5 and brings the queen to a5 early: an offbeat, aggressive system.
opening.family.pterodactyl.advanced = A mix of Modern and Sicilian ideas: the queen on a5 hits the knight on c3 along the diagonal.
opening.family.bongcloud.beginner = White moves the king on move two: a joke opening that breaks the basic rules, since the king should stay safe and castle.
opening.family.bongcloud.advanced = The king blocks the bishop and the queen and gives up castling; it is played as a joke and should not be imitated.

# ---- Notable variations: advanced comments (levels 4-6), and beginner ones where a beginner meets the line
opening.variation.open_sicilian.advanced = White opens the centre with d4 and recaptures with the knight: the main battleground of the Sicilian, sharp and full of theory.
opening.variation.closed_sicilian.advanced = White avoids d4, fianchettoes on g2 and often plays f4, aiming for a slow kingside attack.
opening.variation.alapin.advanced = c3 prepares d4 to build a full pawn centre and sidestep the main Sicilian theory; Black answers with d5 or the knight to f6.
opening.variation.smith_morra.beginner = White gives a pawn to develop quickly and open lines against Black's king: Black must develop carefully.
opening.variation.smith_morra.advanced = A pawn for rapid development and the open c-file and d-file; Black develops soundly and often returns the pawn at the right moment.
opening.variation.grand_prix.advanced = White plays the knight to c3 and f4 for a quick kingside attack with f5, avoiding the Open Sicilian.
opening.variation.rossolimo.advanced = The bishop on b5 pins or trades the knight on c6 to give Black doubled pawns and keep the game positional.
opening.variation.najdorf.advanced = a6 makes the most famous Sicilian: flexible and deep, with e5 or e6 structures against White's sharp attacks.
opening.variation.dragon.advanced = Black fianchettoes on g7; the Yugoslav Attack leads to famous races between attacks on opposite wings.
opening.variation.accelerated_dragon.advanced = Black fianchettoes before d6 to play d5 in one go; the Maróczy Bind with c4 is White's positional antidote.
opening.variation.scheveningen.advanced = Black builds the small centre with e6 and d6, a flexible defensive shell; White often attacks with g4.
opening.variation.sveshnikov.advanced = Black plays e5, accepting a hole on d5 and a backward d-pawn for activity and the bishop pair: very dynamic.
opening.variation.taimanov.advanced = Black combines e6 and the knight on c6 with a flexible a6 and the queen on c7, keeping d6 in reserve.
opening.variation.kan.advanced = Black plays e6 and a6, keeps the knights at home and often aims for a Hedgehog setup.
opening.variation.classical_sicilian.advanced = Black develops both knights and plays d6; White's main answers are the Richter-Rauzer and the Sozin.
opening.variation.french_advance.advanced = White locks the centre with e5 and gains space; Black attacks d4, the base of the chain, with c5 and pieces.
opening.variation.french_exchange.advanced = The pawn exchange on d5 releases the tension: a symmetrical, quiet structure in which Black's light-squared bishop is freed.
opening.variation.french_tarrasch.advanced = The knight goes to d2 to avoid the Winawer pin; Black often accepts an isolated d-pawn after c5, or locks the centre.
opening.variation.winawer.advanced = The bishop on b4 pins the knight; Black usually gives bishop for knight, leaving White doubled pawns but the bishop pair.
opening.variation.french_classical.advanced = Black develops the knight to f6; the play turns on the e5 pawn chain and the pin by a bishop on g5.
opening.variation.french_rubinstein.advanced = Black takes on e4 and gives up the centre for a solid, compact position, aiming for c5 later.
opening.variation.ck_advance.advanced = White gains space with e5; Black develops the bishop to f5 before e6 and fights back with c5.
opening.variation.ck_exchange.advanced = A Carlsbad structure with the colours reversed: White sets up on the kingside, Black plays for a minority attack on the queenside.
opening.variation.ck_panov.advanced = White plays c4 and accepts an isolated d-pawn in exchange for active pieces and attacking chances.
